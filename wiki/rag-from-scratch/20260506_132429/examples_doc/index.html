📚 What is this & why does it exist?

examples/ is the actual product of this repository. src/ exists as a side-effect of stabilising what the lessons have proven. Every numbered directory is a self-contained, runnable lesson. A learner can read one lesson without having read any other — that is the central design contract.

💡 Real-world analogy

A driving instructor's lesson plan: the first lesson teaches turning the wheel without ever explaining gear ratios. The fifth lesson explains the clutch without re-deriving inertia. Each lesson assumes you remember the previous; none assumes you've read the engineering textbook. The textbook (src/) is on the back shelf for the curious; the lessons (examples/) are the road.

🎯 Why This Module Exists

The lessons exist because RAG appears intimidating in the LangChain era but stops being intimidating once you have written the keyword-scorer, the splitter, the embedding cache, and the rank-fusion equation yourself. The author's wager is that direct hands-on contact with each primitive removes the magic; the curriculum is built around that wager.

The lessons are not a thin demo of the src/ library. They are the source of truth for what should eventually live in src/. Lesson 03 re-implements its own splitter; lesson 06/04 ships a multi-query-retrieval.js helper that has not yet been promoted. Reading the lessons in order, you watch the abstractions emerge.

🚶 Step-by-Step Narrated Walkthrough

The reader's path from "what is RAG?" to "I can build my own retrieval strategy":

  1. Lesson 00 (How RAG Works) — 68 LOC of pure JS. Knowledge is an array of strings. naiveKeywordSearch() scores each doc by word overlap. generateAnswer() concatenates retrieved docs (no LLM). The reader sees retrieve+generate work; the failures (e.g., "What is the weather today?") motivate every later lesson.
  2. Lessons 01/01 + 01/02 (LLM basics) — loads a GGUF model with node-llama-cpp; wraps it in a class. The reader learns that "an LLM" is just a function from string to string.
  3. Lesson 02 (Data Loading) — uses PDFLoader from src/ (already promoted). Demonstrates page-split vs single-document modes.
  4. Lesson 03 (Splitting) — re-implements TextSplitter inline (cognitive locality!). Compares character-window, sentence-aware, and recursive strategies on the same PDF.
  5. Lesson 04/01 (Similarity basics) — embeds 5 sample sentences, computes pairwise cosine similarity, prints a heatmap. Reader sees "these two sentences are 0.82 similar".
  6. Lesson 04/02 (Generate embeddings) — scales up to a real document set. Introduces caching as a side-channel.
  7. Lesson 05/01 (In-memory store) — constructs a VectorDB from embedded-vector-db. CRUD operations, similarity search, metadata storage.
  8. Lesson 05/02 (Nearest-neighbour search) — introduces HNSW parameters (ef_construction, ef_search, M). Shows the speed/precision knob.
  9. Lesson 05/03 (Metadata filtering) — where clauses on metadata; pre-filter vs post-filter tradeoffs.
  10. Lesson 06/01 (Basic retrieval) — the canonical retrieve-then-generate loop end to end.
  11. Lesson 06/02 (Query preprocessing) — lowercase, strip stop-words, expand contractions. Heuristic-only; no LLM yet.
  12. Lesson 06/03 (Hybrid search) — combine dense (cosine) + sparse (BM25) scores. Min-max normalisation. Dynamic weighting based on query patterns (regex SKU detection bumps sparse weight).
  13. Lesson 06/04 (Multi-query) — retrieveParallel(queries) → rrfFuse(lists). Reciprocal Rank Fusion at k=60. Promotion candidate for src/retrievers/.
  14. Lesson 06/05 (Query rewriting) — LLM rewrites the user query into 1–3 paraphrases; feed to multi-query.

📊 Performance Profile

The lessons are interactive demos — cold-start dominates everything. Loading bge-small-en-v1.5.Q8_0.gguf (~33 MB) takes ~500 ms; loading a 1B-parameter generation model takes 1–3 s. Once warm, embedding throughput is ~30 docs/s on a modern CPU; HNSW search over 10K vectors is sub-ms; LLM generation runs at 5–30 tok/s depending on the model and quantisation.

The lessons trade speed for clarity. OutputHelper.withSpinner wraps long ops; OutputHelper.runExample reports total elapsed; chalk/ora ensure the reader sees what's happening. None of this matters for production performance — it matters for educational pacing.
